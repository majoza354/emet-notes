# One loop

## The one question

Every row in every one of the six tables is asked one question when it arrives: is this new. Entity, fact, edge, aspect, value, edge type. The question is the same. The table it lands in is not a decision anyone makes; the passport already knows.

What counts as new is not one test. It is as specific to the schema, entity type and aspect as the consequences of a landing are. An entity is new by identity. An aspect is new by label. A value is new by the fold. Each is a rule on the row, scoped by the same three words. is_new is not an executor. It is a word on the a_type, beside folds and lands.

## The loop

Unwrap. For every line, is_new. Every line that is new goes to the drain and is written. Nobody decides anything on the way in.

After the write, the fold. The fold is what the write means: the new standing after this observation joins the stream. If the fold did not move, nothing happened. An unchanged value is not a landing, and that is now the fold returning the same answer, not a separate check.

After the fold, the alert. The hopper fires on every write to every one of the six tables. That firing is the alert. The machine is not told; it is downstream of the trigger by construction, and it is woken with the folded standing, not the raw row.

Then the machine looks up what this kind of landing owes. Consequences are rows. One landing, many consequences, each one a job. Each job's write goes back through is_new, write, fold, alert.

There is no separate outbound path. There is one loop, and the machine is what makes it loop.

## The machine

The engine moves things and never looks at them. The machine looks at things en route: recognises, judges, shapes, decides where a thing may go. It is the second half of the loop. Compliance is its name in the drain today.

Compliance is not border patrol. It routes: go here, go there, no entry. Refusal is one of the things it can say, not the only thing.

Two lookups, keyed differently, which is why they are two.

What to do with this. Keyed by the source: this schema, this entity type, this aspect landed, and it owes these destinations.

The rules for where it is going. Keyed by the pair: this source to this destination. What it turns into on the way, and whether it is allowed at all.

What happens to a value on the way belongs to the pair, not to the source and not to the destination. A text becoming a decimal is neither side's business alone. So the how word leaves the source's lands entry and lives in the machine, keyed by source and destination. The row carries three words about behaviour: what counts as new, how to fold, where it goes. The fourth lives with the pair.

Compliance decides whether. Rows decide what. Neither borrows the other's job.

## Direction is not a property

The path from a marketplace listing to an ops allocation is not a different direction from the path from an ops allocation to a marketplace listing. Direction was an accident of which side arrived first.

Listing lands, ops allocation is a consequence. Allocation lands, listing is a consequence. Same lookup, same machine, different pair. A lot dropped in from the keyboard is an arrival that happened not to come from a marketplace.

This is why blocker one existed. Resolve had inbound baked into its edge rule, so outbound looked like a reversal. Under this there is nothing to reverse.

## Inside out

A destination declares its interest. Ops says it wants this, once, at configuration time, and that declaration becomes the row. The mechanism does not change; who authors the entry does.

Land everything that arrives. Every field, into the gear it arrived in, whether or not anyone wants it yet. An aspect with no home is minted as an empty a_type: label, entity type, dtype, and nothing else. Folds null, no lands. When someone wants it, the words are filled in. The history is already there.

The five thousand null-fold a_types are not a defect under this. They are the shape working.

Backfill is free. The hopper stands on all six tables, so adding a lands entry to an a_type is a write with consequences like any other, and the replay queues itself. No routine.

## Resolve reduced

Resolve today does five things: recognition, parentage, edge, face, and the chain to the next passport, tangled rather than stacked. Under this it does nothing before the drain has cleared the write. It is a scribe: write the row, draw the edge the row names, write the face.

Mint or bind is a false pair. is_new answers with a uuid or nothing. If nothing, mint. Bind is the absence of an action, not an action.

Parent is a word on the birth row: root, strip, or twin of parent. Resolve reads the word and transports nothing. No rule says "the source's parent", so the reversed parent cannot happen. The listing's parent is the tenant root; the allocation's is the lot; the lot's is the item; the item's is the form; the line's is the twin of its order. Each gear states its own parentage from what it knows locally. The two parents were never the same fact, so there was never anything to carry across the twin.

Parent stays a column. The law is a rule about birth, not about storage; the mint refuses to complete without one.

Twinning is lateral and writes a has_twin edge, never a parent. Birth is vertical and writes a parent, never an edge. One rule per direction, never both on one act.

## Recognition as a ladder

Rung one is the index lookup scoped to the space. It stays, because that is where the tenant wall is enforced on identity.

When rung one misses, the harder rungs are rows, scoped by schema and entity type, differing by case: a cross-marketplace code for a product, a picture for an item or form. They come out of resolve and into the machine.

The harder rungs are a clock boundary, not a third answer in the moment. A picture match is an outgoing call whose reply lands later. Review is the state between the ask and the answer. Whether the entity is minted at the miss and carries a review NO until the answer, or the job stands unpassed until the machine answers, is the open question in ASK #115603.

## Executors that remain

Rows say what happens. Executors do the three things rows cannot: recognise beyond the index, compute a set, translate at the exit.

Election is the second of these. A lot's qualified marketplaces are a join the tenant configured, division by marketplace, read up the strip to the form. The consequence list is rows, but the qualifying set is computed. A consequence can be a set an executor returns, not only a fixed array. Naming that keeps blocker two from returning under another name.

Ending an eBay listing and archiving a Woo listing are the third. Same fact, two spellings at the exit. Compose's job, not a transform.

## Open

Job granularity. Receive today unwraps a whole package inside one job. If every new row became its own drain job, one arrival becomes a hundred jobs. is_new and compliance should run per row inside one job per package; to_queue already folds one job per entity. This has to be said or the pure version is slower by an order.

Entities via the drain, and qualify-then-mint against mint-then-qualify. ASK #115603, pending.

Replay mechanics for rows that landed before the rule that now wants them. The hopper on six tables says it queues itself; how the replay is bounded is not yet said.

The name for the machine. Provisional.

Compliance as a load-bearing router. Today it never fires. Tomorrow every route lives in it. It stays honest only if its rows are keyed by the pair and nothing in it is code.

## Already built, and new

Already built and unwired: the hopper on three of six tables; leftovers landed as unclaimed; the how word; op and says; compare before write; the election ladvar as a row; the cue rows under the passport.

New: recognition out of resolve; the parent word on the birth row; the birth branch reading election; compliance that routes; the hopper on the three vocabulary tables; transforms keyed by the pair, resident in the machine; the fold before the alert.

Six changes and one structural question.
